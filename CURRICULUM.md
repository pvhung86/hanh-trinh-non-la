# Hành Trình Nón Lá — Heritage Curriculum Map

**Primary learners:** Children aged 5–15 growing up in Vietnamese-speaking families overseas. The game assumes a range of starting points: some children may understand family conversation but need support speaking, expanding vocabulary, reading tone marks or speaking with confidence.

**Target variety:** Northern Vietnamese with a Hà Nội reference model. The game requests `vi-VN` from browser speech services, but the actual speech-recognition and text-to-speech voice depends on the device/provider and is not guaranteed to be a Hà Nội voice.

| Stage | Levels | Age guide | Communication outcomes | Language and cultural focus |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Family Greetings | 1–15 | 5–7 | Greet relatives, respond politely, express needs, introduce close family and talk about using Vietnamese at home. | Kinship terms, `con`, `ông bà`, `bố mẹ`, `anh/chị/em`, `vâng ạ`, `ạ`. |
| Home & Everyday Life | 16–30 | 6–9 | Describe routines, ask for help, talk about meals and preferences, take part in household activities and contact relatives. | Food, chores, family meals, home vocabulary, common tone patterns and final consonants. |
| Friends & Community | 31–45 | 8–10 | Talk about school, games and feelings; ask questions; request clarification; share family traditions with peers. | Conversation strategies, question forms, feelings, peer language, Tết as a family topic. |
| Stories & Visits | 46–60 | 9–11 | Recount a visit, sequence events, describe shared meals and memories, explain a school cultural activity and make future plans. | Time markers, sequencing, past/future reference, narrative linking and family storytelling. |
| Language & Culture | 61–80 | 11–13 | Explain family customs, forms of address, regional vocabulary and the role of tone marks; describe how heritage language is maintained. | Tết, intergenerational language, Northern polite forms, particles `ạ` and `nhé`, six tones, pronunciation awareness and regional respect. |
| Heritage Storyteller | 81–100 | 13–15 | Reflect on identity, explain ideas, compare traditions, interpret proverbs, present a viewpoint and tell a family story for another generation. | Extended sentences, argument, comparison, storytelling, proverbs and personal reflection. |

## Pronunciation approach

The game introduces the six Hà Nội tone categories as a listening-and-repetition aid:

- **Ngang:** level and steady.
- **Huyền:** low and falling.
- **Sắc:** high and rising.
- **Hỏi:** dipping and turning upward.
- **Ngã:** a distinctive broken/creaky quality in careful Hà Nội speech.
- **Nặng:** short, low and constricted.

The notes also highlight some Northern sound patterns, including the z-like sound used for `d` and `gi` (and often `r`) in Hà Nội speech; `đ` is a different initial consonant. Children should listen to family members and teachers and should not be told that another regional variety is incorrect.

## Feedback principles

1. **Encourage attempts.** A mismatch from browser speech recognition is not presented as proof of poor pronunciation.
2. **Show the transcript.** When available, the app displays what the speech service heard.
3. **Coach a next step.** Learners are invited to listen again, practise in smaller parts and try the whole phrase.
4. **Keep a writing route.** Typed practice remains available when microphone access or recognition is unavailable.
5. **Do not score accent.** The browser recogniser is used as a simple game trigger, not a clinical, educational or accent assessment.
6. **Respect family language.** Children can discuss family-specific alternatives with relatives; the target phrase is a practice model, not a judgement of how every household must speak.

## Curriculum reference

Vietnam's Ministry of Education and Training Circular 28/2018/TT-BGDDT provides a Vietnamese curriculum for overseas Vietnamese learners, structured into three proficiency levels and six stages, with listening and speaking skills emphasised. This game adopts the broad heritage-learning orientation and a six-stage progression, but its 100 short game levels are an adaptation rather than an accredited or one-to-one implementation.
